# Models

The 3D mode's models are generated by the Blender scripts in this directory and written to `packages/game/public/models` as GLB. Nothing here is hand-edited in the Blender UI, so every model can be rebuilt:

```sh
tools/models/bl tools/models/hippo/build.py
```

`bl` runs Blender headless (set `BLENDER` to its path if it is not in `~/.local/opt`). `common.py` has the helpers every script uses: `reset`, `part`, `empty`, `export_glb`, `report`, `preview`. `preview` re-opens the exported GLB and renders it in a dark room under one flashlight, into `tools/models/previews` (not committed).

## The contract with the game

Sizes are metres. In Blender the model stands on z = 0, centred on the origin, facing -Y. The exporter turns that into three.js axes: +Y up, facing +Z.

A model is a tree of rigid mesh parts with no armature. The game animates parts by name (`packages/game/public/src/three/`), so the names, the pivots and the rest pose are the interface:

- Every part's origin is the joint it turns about. Rotation and scale are applied (identity at rest).
- Parts overlap at the joints by enough that a 40 degree turn opens no gap.
- A positive turn about a part's local X pitches something that points forward down (a jaw opens), and swings something that hangs down toward the back.
- `_L` is the model's own left, which is +X.

Mascot parts:

| Node | Pivot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `root` | origin, on the floor | an empty; the only top-level node |
| `body` | pelvis | torso, hips and the rear. Parent of everything below |
| `neck` | base of the neck | optional |
| `head` | where the head meets the neck | child of `neck` if there is one |
| `jaw` | the hinge | child of `head`. Optional if the mouth cannot open |
| `eye_L`, `eye_R` | centre of the eyeball | children of `head`. The game turns them to look at a player |
| `arm_L`, `arm_R` | shoulder | optional children `forearm_L`, `forearm_R` at the elbow |
| `leg_L`, `leg_R` | hip | optional children `shin_L`, `shin_R` at the knee |
| `extra_N` | where it joins | anything else that moves on a joint: extra limbs, a tail. Chains nest: `extra_1`, child `extra_1_2`, child `extra_1_3` |
| `sway_N` | where it hangs from | soft hanging things the game swings: hair, guts, torn cloth |

Materials named `eye_glow` are drawn emissive and unlit by the game, so they show in the dark. Any other material is ordinary PBR.

Each mascot has three files, `<key>_0.glb`, `<key>_1.glb`, `<key>_2.glb`, one per rot stage, and a `<key>.json` beside them that tells the game what it cannot read from the file (see any of them for the fields).

Limits per file: 70k triangles, 3 MB, textures at most 2048 px. Mascots at stages 0 and 1 fit in a box 2.3 m wide, 1.9 m deep and 2.7 m tall; stage 2 may be 3.3 m tall and 2.6 m wide. Doorways in the building are 2.6 m wide and 3 m high.

## Building a model

Models are seen close up, from below, by one warm torch beam with everything else black. That decides what matters.

- Run Blender only through `bl`. Blender 5.3's Python API differs from older versions; when a call fails, inspect it from inside Blender (`dir()`, `bl_rna.properties`) before guessing again.
- Write only in your own script directory here and your own files in `packages/game/public/models`. Scripts are the source of the model: rebuildable from scratch with fixed seeds, commented with the why, no leftover experiments.
- You cannot see a model unless you render it. After every change, run `preview(name, ...)` and look at the PNGs. Use `light='studio'` for shape and texture, `focus=((x, y, z), radius)` for close-ups, and the `low` view for what a player at its feet sees. Run `report(name)` and check the node tree, pivots, triangle count and bounds against the contract above. Pose-test the joints (turn head, jaw and limbs 30 to 40 degrees in a throwaway script and render) before calling a stage done.
- Keep the three or four best previews of each stage in `shots/` in your script directory, so a model can be reviewed without rebuilding it.

What "detailed and scary" means here:

- Surface. Bake procedural materials (colour with AO folded in, normal, roughness) into image textures, or use dense vertex colour. Blender shader nodes do not export to glTF unless baked. Matted fake fur, stitched seams, a zip, foam showing through tears, scratched plastic eyes, yellowed teeth, wet stains.
- Form. Sculpted masses that flow into each other (metaballs or a voxel remesh, then subdivide, displace and decimate to budget), not primitives parked side by side. Hair and strands are geometry where they show in silhouette.
- Wrongness before gore. Proportions slightly off, eyes that do not agree, a mouth too wide, human teeth in a plush face. The last stage adds the gore the old 2D art had (`public/src/render/mascot.js`, removed; see git history).
- The torch test. The face has to read when lit from beside the camera, and the `eye_glow` eyes have to show as two points in the dark.
- The hips. At stages 0 and 1 every mascot has enormous hips and a huge rear, wider than tall. It is the joke in the game's title. Stage 2 drops the joke: the rear is small and wasted, no wider than the shoulders, so the nightmare form reads as starved rather than comic.
